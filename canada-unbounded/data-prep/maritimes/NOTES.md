### 1a. The Irvings offshore (documented)
Source for this section: the CBC/Radio-Canada Paradise Papers series by Jacques Poitras and Frédéric Zalac, Nov 2022 [secondary, based on leaked Appleby documents and Bermuda court filings].
- **K.C. Irving left NB at the end of 1971**, "days before a new provincial estate tax came into force". He lived briefly in the Bahamas, then settled in Bermuda (one piece says he moved there in 1972), and stayed a Bermuda resident until his death in 1992. In a 2017 letter to *Atlantic Business Magazine*, his son J.K. said the tax changes could have meant a rate of up to 80% and "would have been devastating to the businesses". Tax law professor Geoffrey Loomer (UVic) called the 80% figure "a stretch". https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6628921
- **Bermuda holding companies:** F.M.O. (1968), F.M.P. and Forest Mere Investments (1969), and at least 11 "F.M." companies in all. F.M.O. was the parent of Irving Oil and F.M.P. the parent of Irving Pulp & Paper. StatCan's inter-corporate ownership database listed the NB Irving companies under eight Bermuda holding companies while showing them under "effective control" from Canada. https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6628921 and https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6639615
- **F.M.A. Ltd. (Bermuda captive insurer, 1973):** it accumulated "almost $13.4 million in untaxed income" between 1973 and 2001. JDI says captive insurance "continues to be a good business approach" and that sales were at fair market value. The practice is legal. https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6628921
- **K.C. Irving Estate Trust (Bermuda):** worth "$3 billion" when its split was approved in 2010. Its trustees were non-residents (his widow in Bermuda and lawyers in Bermuda and New York), which experts said made it non-taxable in Canada. The family raced to restructure before federal non-resident trust rules took effect at the end of 2013. Two 1976 trusts were declared void by the Bermuda Supreme Court on 22 March 2010, which avoided a large tax bill. Legal costs were about $100M. In 1980 testimony Arthur Irving said of a $4M gift to a trust: "It was good business, tax reasons." https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6639780
- **Today:** Bermuda's registry says all known Irving companies on the island have been dissolved, the last (F.M.K.) in May 2021. JDI says there is "no offshore ownership or control of our organization". However, JDI's federal lobbyist registration listed F.M.W. (Bermuda) as its parent until October 2021. Irish filings showed the Bermuda-based "ALI Family No. 1 Trust" as Irving Oil's ultimate parent as of 31 Dec 2020. Irving Oil says "the full ownership structure of Irving Oil is fully taxable in Canada". Arthur and Sandra Irving were directors of two Barbados companies (Bianca Ltd., Seagrape Holdings). https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6639615
- **NB government:** in 2019 the finance minister told the legislature, "We do not know ... We would have no way of finding that out" about revenue lost to Irving use of tax havens. https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6639615
- **No illegality has been found.** Every arrangement above is described as legal tax avoidance.

### 1b. Tax litigation
- ***Irving Oil Ltd. v. M.N.R.* (FCA, 18 Feb 1991), (1991) 126 N.R. 47, 91 DTC 5106, [1991] 1 CTC 350; Mahoney, Hugessen, MacGuigan JJ.A.** Irving Oil was then owned 50/50 by Standard Oil of California (Socal) and the Irvings. It bought crude through its Bermuda subsidiary IrvCal, which bought from Socal at cost and resold to Irving at fair market value. The Minister disallowed $141,946,190 in deductions for 1971–75. Irving won at trial (Muldoon J., 1988), the FCA dismissed the Crown's appeal, and the SCC refused leave. [primary-summary: vLex headnote https://ca.vlex.com/vid/irving-oil-ltd-v-680561157]. CBC quotes Mahoney J.A.: the transactions were "artificial" and "a tax avoidance scheme, pure and simple", but "the tax avoidance scheme contrived in the present case did not offend the Income Tax Act". CBC also reports IrvCal made $142M from 1971 to 1975, of which $137M went to Irving Oil as tax-free dividends [secondary: https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6628921]. **I could not read the full judgment (CanLII blocks fetches); check the quote against 91 DTC 5106 before publishing.** Correct name: "Canada v. Irving Oil" is the appeal styling; the reported name is *Irving Oil Ltd. v. M.N.R.*
- *Irving Oil Ltd. v. M.N.R.*, 2001 FCA 364: a minor dispute over whether interest on a tax refund counted as business income. Irving won [primary-summary: https://ca.vlex.com/vid/irving-oil-ltd-v-680598641]. Not significant for the essay.
- In 1975 the province took Irving Oil to the Supreme Court of Canada over taxing the tank farm, and won [secondary: CBC 2016, https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3889095].
- **McCain / Sobey:** I found no documented offshore structures or tax disputes. I did not search exhaustively, so the essay should neither claim nor imply any.

## 2. Resource terms

### 2a. NB Crown forest
- **JDI is the largest Crown licensee.** In 2019 J.D. Irving Ltd held 1,046,640 ha of the 3,272,505 ha of licensed or department-managed Crown forest, which is **32.0%**. The other licensees are Fornebu (Norway's Umoe Group) with 948,916 ha, AV Nackawic/AV Cell (India's Aditya Birla Group) with 678,955 ha, and Twin Rivers (Maine) with 527,179 ha [primary: AGNB 2020 Vol I Ch 4, Exhibit 4.2, p.184, https://www.agnb-vgnb.ca/uploads/volume_section_translations/319/file/Chap4e.pdf]. The 2013 figures match (Fundy licence 1,046,967 ha, 32%) [primary: AGNB 2015 Vol II Ch 3, Exhibit 3.3, p.126]. **Fairness point:** JDI is the only large Crown licensee headquartered in the Maritimes. The other two-thirds of licensed Crown forest is held by firms based in Norway, India and Maine.
- **JDI freehold:** "J.D. Irving, Ltd., alone owns 725,000 hectares" of NB's roughly 3 million ha of private forest [secondary: CBC 2016, https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3887534]. Land Report gives the family 3.2M acres across Maine, NB and NS [secondary]. *Derived, approximate:* if Crown land is about 50% of NB forest and that 50% is the ~3.27M ha above, JDI owns or holds licences over roughly 1.77M ha, or **about a quarter or more of NB forest land**. This is my calculation. Label it "roughly" and show the inputs.
- **Ownership of NB forest land:** Crown 50%, private woodlots 30%, industrial freehold 18%, federal 2% (2019) [primary: AGNB 2020, Exhibit 4.1].
- **The 2014 forestry strategy and the JDI agreement.** The Alward government's March 2014 plan raised the allowable softwood cut on Crown land "by about 20 per cent, or 660,000 cubic metres". The 25-year JDI agreement raised JDI's annual allocation of spruce, fir, jack pine and white pine to 2,027,000 m³, with at least 1,898,000 m³ of spruce (up from 1,500,000). JDI committed $513M to its mills, $450M of it at Irving Pulp & Paper, with "no federal or provincial funds". [secondary: CBC 13 Mar 2014 https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.2570971; 29 Apr 2014 https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.2625292]
  - Former Liberal DNR minister Wally Stiles rejected a similar JDI proposal: "if I was minister today, I would refuse again."
  - UNB contract-law professor David Bell said the deal puts "very specific obligations" on the province, while "J.D. Irving's obligations ... are only approximate. They are softened considerably", subject to "commercially reasonable" and "market conditions" qualifiers [secondary: CBC 30 Apr 2014, https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.2627236].
  - A UNB survey (Tom Beckley) found more than 60% of New Brunswickers opposed the plan, while an Irving-commissioned poll found slight support [secondary: CBC via search; https://amp.cbc.ca/news/canada/new-brunswick/irving-sponsored-poll-finds-slight-support-for-forest-deal-1.2696140].
- **Auditor General 2015 Vol II, Ch 3 (Silviculture)** [primary: https://www.agnb-vgnb.ca/uploads/volume_section_translations/147/file/Chap3e.pdf]
  - The AG's own statement of operations (Exhibit 3.10, unaudited, p.148): Crown royalties of $64.8M (2013), less $26.5M in licence management fees and $24.6M in Crown silviculture, left **operating earnings of only $4.9M (2013), $3.4M (2012), $1.5M (2011) and −$10.5M (2010)**. After fire, pest and private silviculture costs, the **forest management "deficit" was −$7.6M (2013), −$9.5M (2012), −$10.7M (2011), −$20.6M (2010) and −$5.3M (2009)**.
  - ¶3.153: "The net contribution to the Department after operating costs including Crown silviculture is insufficient to cover the other forest management commitments made by the Department."
  - ¶3.151: the AG's 2001 recommendation to report results "in a more business-like way" was meant "to potentially address public concerns that the Department was 'giving away' the Crown resources". It "has not been fully implemented".
  - ¶3.28: silviculture is not bought competitively. Prices are "set by the Department using a costing model with no competitive open market influence", and licensees are "the sole source providers on Crown land".
  - ¶3.154–3.157: no expectation of a direct financial return. The benefits are indirect (jobs: the department estimated 668 jobs a year for $25M a year in spending).
  - Exhibit 3.11: JDI received the largest share of silviculture payments, roughly in line with its 32% share of productive Crown forest.
  - **Caution:** the AG did not say Irving was overpaid or that royalties were below market. The finding is that the Crown forest nets the province roughly break-even to negative once management costs are counted, and that the department did not track value for money.
- **Auditor General 2015 Vol II, Ch 4 (Private Wood Supply)** [primary: https://www.agnb-vgnb.ca/uploads/volume_section_translations/151/file/Chap4e.pdf]
  - ¶4.14: the department "does not meet its principal responsibilities under legislation respecting timber supply from private forest lands" and "does not comply with legislated requirements under the Crown Lands and Forests Act". The Act requires proportional supply, and ¶4.71 onward says that has not been reported on since at least 2002.
  - ¶4.80 and Exhibit 4.5: from 2004-05 to 2012-13 the private woodlot share of mill consumption fell from **23% to 12%**, while the Crown share rose from **41% to 51%**. See `nb_wood_consumption_by_source.csv`.
- **Auditor General 2020 Vol I, Ch 4 (Follow-up: 2008 Timber Royalties)** [primary, same URL as above]
  - The 2008 audit (quoted at ¶4.33): "the royalties do not reflect fair market value – in some instances the royalty rates appear below market rates and in other instances they appear to be above."
  - 2020 conclusion: the rate-setting process has "significantly improved", but the department "has yet to adjust and apply new rates as required under the Crown Lands and Forests Act since 2015". Royalties fell from $76.7M (2015) to $67.9M (2019), down about 11%.
  - The forestry sector employs 22,000 people and makes up 5% of NB GDP.
  - **So "royalties are set below market" is not established.** What is established: the rates were found not to reflect fair market value (in both directions) in 2008, and they were not updated as the law requires from 2015 to 2019.
- Follow-up, Feb 2021: AG Kim Adair-MacPherson said the department "continues to disregard the need to reduce clear-cut harvest on Crown land" [secondary: Conservation Council NB, https://www.conservationcouncil.ca/then-and-now-what-the-ag-says-about-forest-management-in-n-b/].

### 2b. Irving Oil property tax (Saint John)
**Correction:** I found no 1980s fixed-assessment law for the refinery itself and no 2021 change to one. The documented items are listed below. The fixed-tax special legislation was the **2005 Canaport LNG Act**, repealed in 2016 (effective for 2017).
- **Canaport crude tank farm, provincial property tax exemption (Assessment Act), adopted 1980 under the Hatfield government** to help Irving Oil through the 1979 oil crisis. The statute does not name Irving. It covers crude storage tanks "used solely for supplying crude oil to the oil refinery". The 1980 estimate was $400k–$500k a year. **NB Finance's tax expenditure report valued it at $674,929 for 2021**, and CBC estimates it saved Irving Oil "more than $20 million" over 42 years. Property records switched the tank farm to "fully taxable" for 2023, unexplained, but the province said the exemption had not been repealed. Municipal tax was always paid. [secondary: CBC 2023-03-09, https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6772467; CBC 2016-12-12, https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3889095]. *Primary to check:* NB Department of Finance Tax Expenditure Report (2021/2022 edition).
- **Canaport LNG:** in 2005 Saint John capped the terminal's tax at $500,000 a year until 2030, which needed provincial legislation (the Lord government). The estimated cost to the city was $112M over 25 years. Leaked documents show Irving Oil earned $250.7M in profit in 2005. The cap was repealed with all-party support in 2016, effective 2017. A separate 1997 provincial port exemption made about $6.5M a year in provincial tax on the LNG land "uncollectable", and it was still in place in 2016. [secondary: CBC https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6646387 and https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3887534]. Irving Oil sold its stake to Repsol in 2021.
- **Refinery assessment:** the main refinery property was assessed at $104.4M (2022) and $107M (2023), up only about $10M in 10 years despite more than $500M in work since 2014. A promised review of the refinery's assessment was cancelled in 2022 after Service NB said the assessment "falls within the acceptable range". About $6M in property tax was collected in 2020 on the refinery and Canaport. A Resources for the Future study implies about US$23M for a US refinery of the same capacity. The comparison is CBC's, built on RFF's average of 19 large US refineries (Raimi). Irving says its "per barrel taxes paid are much higher than our competition" among Canadian refineries. [secondary: CBC 2022-07-04 https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6509171; 2022-12-02 https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6671058]. NB caps the heavy-industry municipal rate at 1.7 times the residential rate.
- **Context:** CBC (2016) estimated that all NB property tax concessions cost about $380M over nearly 40 years, about $24M in 2016. These are not all Irving's: they include farmland deferral (where McCain sheltered "at least $11 million" of farm property in 2016) and the forest-land assessment freeze at $100/ha since 1994, which benefits all private forest owners including JDI. https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3887534 and https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3889406
- **Other public support (mentioned by CBC, not itemised):** "$304 million in loans to J.D. Irving Ltd.'s Irving Shipbuilding in Halifax" [secondary: https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.6628921].

### 2c. PEI land
CBC found more than 12,600 acres owned by about 20 Irving-linked corporations, "just under one per cent" of PEI. The Lands Protection Act caps a corporation at 3,000 acres (5,700 with allowances), with anti-stacking rules. In 2020 the minister said IRAC found "reasonable and probable grounds" of aggregate holdings over the limits and ordered divestiture. In 2022 the province said compliance had been achieved, without explanation, and title records show the land was not sold. "None of this is to say that any single Irving-controlled corporation violates the law." JDI says it is fully compliant. [secondary: CBC 2024-10-31 https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7368416; 2020-11-25 https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5814490]

## 3. Market position
- **Forestry:** JDI holds 32% of licensed Crown forest and about 725,000 ha of freehold (see 2a). Crown wood was 51% of NB mill consumption in 2012-13 (AGNB). I found no published figure for JDI's share of total NB harvest or mill capacity. Do not invent one.
- **Fuel:** Irving Oil runs Canada's largest refinery, about 15% of national capacity in 2009 per the leaked placement memo [secondary: CBC 1.6646387].
- **Media:** already covered in c07 (sold to Postmedia in 2022).
- **Grocery:** Empire/Sobeys is one of five grocers holding about 80% of the market (Competition Bureau 2023; already cited in c08). Its business is national, with stores in all 10 provinces. Its wealth comes mostly from markets *outside* the Maritimes, so the "rests on Maritime markets" claim does not fit the Sobeys.
- **McCain:** global frozen-potato business in 160 countries. Its raw material is grown partly in NB and PEI, but its revenue is overwhelmingly from outside the Maritimes. The "rests on Maritime resources/markets" claim is weak for McCain beyond its origins.

**Historical framing:** the Maritimes have been Canada's poorest region since roughly the 1920s (the Maritime Rights Movement era). Deindustrialization after about 1900 is the subject of a large literature: T.W. Acheson, "The National Policy and the Industrialization of the Maritimes, 1880–1910", *Acadiensis* 1(2), 1972, 3–28 (https://journals.lib.unb.ca/index.php/Acadiensis/article/view/11300); E.R. Forbes, *The Maritime Rights Movement 1919–1927* (1979). [Bibliographic details verified. The summary of Acheson's thesis (consolidation by central Canadian capital) is from my own knowledge and I could not retrieve the full text, so mark it unverified.] That literature points to central Canadian capital and federal policy, **not to the Irvings, McCains or Sobeys**, whose fortunes grew mostly after the 1920s.

## 6. Scholarly and journalistic assessments
**Critical:**
- Jacques Poitras, *Irving vs. Irving: Canada's Feuding Billionaires and the Stories They Won't Tell* (Viking Canada, 2014). It argues the Irving businesses are underreported partly because of the family's grip on NB newsrooms [secondary: J-Source and an Atlantic Books review by Harvey Sawler, https://atlanticbooks.ca/stories/inside-the-irvings]. Poitras's Nov 2022 CBC/Radio-Canada series (above) is the strongest evidence-based journalism on Irving tax structures.
- Geoffrey Loomer (UVic tax law) on captive insurance: "It's an easy way for a Canadian-based multinational to save some Canadian tax." On JDI's "not owned and controlled from Bermuda" statement: "carefully made, and it's technically true" [CBC].
- Bruce Livesey, six-part National Observer series on the Irvings (2016) [secondary: https://www.nationalobserver.com/node/2166].
- Green MLAs Kevin Arseneau and David Coon (political, not scholarly).

**Defensive / contextual:**
- Jim Irving to an NB legislative committee, Sept 2021: "We're for New Brunswick. This is home ... We're not absentee owners, sitting in New York or Toronto." [CBC 1.6628921]
- JDI: "our companies pay all taxes in Canada"; the group is "wholly owned and controlled by Canadians who proudly reside in New Brunswick". Irving Oil: "the full ownership structure of Irving Oil is fully taxable in Canada." [CBC]
- JDI says it has planted "over 1 billion trees – a national record in Canada" [primary: jdirving.com]. It is NB's largest private employer, with 20,000+ FTE across Canada and the US.
- **Donald Savoie** (Université de Moncton), *Visiting Grandchildren: Economic Development in the Maritimes* (UTP 2006) and *Looking for Bootstraps* (2017). He blames federal policy and Ottawa's central-Canada focus for the region's lag, not local family firms. In "The Maritimes are no longer an economic basket case" (*Globe and Mail*, 6 Aug 2024) he cites "reliance on federal transfers, federal economic policies, and resistance to immigration" and describes a current "remarkable economic resurgence" [secondary: Globe]. **His work does not support the "plunder by local families" thesis.**

## WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS SAYING
1. Two of Canada's four richest families on the Canadian rich list, the **Irvings (C$15.8B) and McCains (C$13.9B, Maclean's 2025)**, are New Brunswick families, and the Sobeys (Nova Scotia) are also billionaires. **New Brunswick has the lowest median household net worth of any province (55% of the national median, SFS 2023)** and the lowest GDP per capita (74.6% of Canada, 2024). NS and NB have the lowest median after-tax incomes.
2. Forbes's 2026 list omits these families mainly because of its rules (dispersed family fortunes are excluded, and fortunes pass to heirs at death). For the Irvings, both patriarchs died in 2024. The omission is not evidence that the wealth shrank.
3. For about four decades the Irvings held a large share of their wealth through Bermuda holding companies and a C$3B Bermuda trust with non-resident trustees, specifically to keep it beyond Canadian tax. K.C. Irving left NB days before a provincial estate tax took effect, which his son later confirmed was tax-motivated. A Federal Court of Appeal judge described the IrvCal arrangement as "a tax avoidance scheme" but held it legal (verify the exact quote). NB's finance minister said the province had "no way of finding" out how much revenue was lost.
4. NB has granted Irving Oil durable, specific tax concessions: the 1980 tank-farm exemption (about $675k a year by 2021, more than $20M cumulatively, still in statute), the 2005 LNG tax cap (about $112M over 25 years, repealed in 2016) and a 1997 provincial exemption (about $6.5M a year on the LNG land in 2016). The refinery's assessment has barely moved in a decade, and its property tax is reportedly about one-fifth of what a comparable US refinery pays.
5. JDI holds licences on about a third of NB's Crown forest and owns about 725,000 ha of freehold. The 2014 deal raised its Crown softwood allocation (spruce minimum from 1.5M to 1.9M m³) on terms a UNB law professor called "softened" for the company. NB's Auditor General found Crown forest management roughly break-even to loss-making for the province after costs (a deficit of −$5M to −$21M a year from 2009 to 2013, by the AG's own statement), silviculture bought without competition, royalties not updated as the law requires since 2015, and the department failing its legal duty on proportional private wood supply.
6. "The province benefits less than it might" is a fair, evidenced claim for NB: a forest that nets little after costs, long-lived concessions, low industrial assessments, and offshore holding structures beyond provincial reach.

## WHAT THE EVIDENCE DOES NOT SUPPORT (corrections to the draft framing)
- **"Criminal":** no court has found any of these families or companies guilty of an offence related to these matters. The 1970s Irving newspaper prosecution ended in acquittal at the SCC (c07). The tax structures were held legal or never challenged. Calling the conduct "criminal" would be inaccurate and likely defamatory. Use "legal", "lawful but aggressive tax avoidance", or "the law permitted it".
- **"Plundered":** not supported as a factual description. Resource access came under licences and statutes passed by elected governments, often at the companies' request. The documented record shows generous terms and weak public oversight, not taking. The government's role (Hatfield 1980, Lord 2005, Alward 2014) is central to the story and should be named.
- **"Domiciled outside the Maritimes":** wrong for the operating companies. JDI and Irving Oil are in Saint John, Empire/Sobeys in Stellarton, McCain in Florenceville-Bristol (plus Toronto), Cavendish Farms in Dieppe. The Irvings say they have exited Bermuda (the registry shows dissolutions by 2021). Irving Oil had a Bermuda-based ultimate-parent trust as of the end of 2020. The accurate phrasing is "held through offshore holding companies and trusts for decades", not "domiciled elsewhere".
- **"Fortunes rest substantially on Maritime resources and markets":** true for the Irvings (NB Crown and freehold forest, the Saint John refinery, NB fuel retail and media), but the Saint John refinery exports about 80% of its output to the US. It is weak for McCain (a global business; Maritime potatoes were its origin) and weak for the Sobeys (Sobeys earns most of its roughly $31B from outside the Maritimes, so it arguably brings national earnings *into* Stellarton).
- **"The Maritimes are *now* the poorest region" as a story of decline:** the region has been Canada's poorest for about a century. On GDP per capita and median income it has *converged* somewhat since 1976–81, though NB's relative net worth has worsened since 2005. Savoie, the leading regional economist, attributes the lag mainly to federal policy, and the historical literature to central Canadian capital, not to these families. The essay can say the families became extraordinarily rich *in* the poorest region, and that the public got modest returns from the resources involved. It should not imply the families caused the region's poverty, which no credible source documents.
- **"Refinery fixed assessment from the 1980s, changed in 2021":** I could not find this. The documented items are the 1980 tank-farm provincial exemption (status changed for 2023 without explanation) and the 2005 LNG tax cap (repealed 2016/17). The refinery is assessed normally but low, and the planned review was cancelled in 2022.
- **"Royalties below market":** not established. The AG (2008) found rates did not reflect fair market value "in some instances ... below ... in other instances ... above". The 2020 finding is a failure to update rates, not underpricing.
- **McCain and Sobey tax and offshore claims:** none documented in this research. Do not group them with the Irvings on offshore matters.
