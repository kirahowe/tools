Sheet Music Scanner

Point your camera at a page of sheet music and hear it played back — entirely in your browser.

What this is

A neural optical-music-recognition system (oemer) running on WebAssembly — Python scientific stack, neural networks and all — plus score rendering and synthesized playback. There is no server: your photo never leaves your device, and after the first visit it works offline.

What you should know before using it

  • It's a big one-time download. Opening the scanner fetches about 175 MB: the recognition models (~109 MB), a Python runtime with numpy/OpenCV/scipy (~50 MB), and the neural-network engine (~15 MB). Everything is cached by your browser, so this happens once, not per scan. Don't do this on a metered connection.
  • It's slow. Recognition takes minutes per page — roughly 3–10 minutes depending on your machine and the engine setting. You'll see live progress the whole way.
  • It's memory-hungry. Expect ~1.5 GB of RAM while recognising. Desktop browsers and recent Android phones handle it; iPhones generally don't.
  • It's imperfect. Clean scans work best; phone photos work decently ("photo mode" straightens curved staff lines). Expect occasional wrong pitches or rhythms, especially on dense scores. Playback is a simple synth — repeats, dynamics and articulations aren't modelled.

What happens if you continue

The moment the scanner page opens, your browser starts downloading the ~175 MB engine and shows its progress. Nothing downloads until then, and nothing is sent anywhere at any point. When the engine is ready, you take or choose a picture, wait out the progress bar, and the recognised score appears — press Play to hear it, with a cursor following along.
